# Historical backtest — first results (2026-11-18)

**Data:** daily adjusted OHLC for SPY, VIX and the 9 original sector SPDRs, 1999-01-04 → 2026-11-17 (7,012 days).
**Method (`sim/backtest.py`):** the same regime classifier and the same fill rules as live trading. Signals use the close of
day t; limit orders are valid for day t+1 only. Stops win ties. Max 25% per position, risk capped at 2% of equity per
trade, 2 bp slippage per fill, long only (the inverse-SPY leg is synthetic before SH existed).
**Split:** tune on TRAIN 1999-2014 → check on VALIDATE 2015-2020 → **TEST 2021-2026 is held out and not yet run.**

## 1. Does the regime label predict returns? Mostly no, and that changed the plan
SPY's average return over the next 20 days was *higher* after "downtrend" days than after "uptrend" days
(train: +0.80% vs +0.55%; validate: +1.85% vs +0.42%). After extreme-volatility downtrend days it was +5.4%
(validate). The classifier describes **risk** well: volatility clusters, and uptrends have smoother returns. It does
not predict **direction**. Going to cash or defensives in downtrends means sitting out the biggest rebounds.
(This diagnostic also printed 2021-26 figures. Nothing was tuned on them.)

## 2. Playbooks compared
| | TRAIN 1999-2014 CAGR / maxDD / Sharpe | VALIDATE 2015-2020 CAGR / maxDD / Sharpe |
|---|---|---|
| SPY buy & hold | +5.1% / −55.2% / 0.35 | **+12.7% / −33.7% / 0.74** |
| v1 = live playbook as of 11-17 (defensive/cash in downtrends) | +1.6% / −9.4% / 0.39 | +0.8% / −5.7% / 0.24 |
| v2 (trend-hold with trailing stops) | +2.9% / −19.9% / 0.38 | +0.1% / −18.6% / 0.05 |
| v3 (buy oversold dips in every regime, trend-hold in calm uptrends) | +5.3% / −29.3% / 0.51 | +3.3% / −16.5% / 0.38 |
| **v3 tuned** (best of 648 combos on TRAIN: 2.5-ATR stop, RSI(2)<15) | **+7.9% / −17.4% / 0.68** | +3.1% / −13.9% / 0.36 |

## 3. What works where (each strategy run ungated, average R by regime at entry, TRAIN)
- **Dip-buying (`range-mean-reversion`)**: positive in 10 of 11 regimes; 67% win rate in both TRAIN (n=913) and VALIDATE (n=252). **This is the one edge that holds out of sample.**
- **`defensive-leader-pullback`**: positive in down regimes (+0.30R to +0.62R, small n). Best standalone Sharpe in VALIDATE (0.80).
- **`inverse-index-trend`** (SH on failed bounces): −0.19R in down/high and negative in VALIDATE. **Dropped.**
- **`trend-hold`**: +0.21R in TRAIN but −0.04R in VALIDATE. Not reliable.
- **`capitulation-bounce`**: big wins, but only 10 trades in 16 years. Promising, too rare to trust.

## 4. Verdict
- **No version yet beats buy-and-hold on a risk-adjusted basis out of sample.** The tuned v3 looks excellent on the
  years it was tuned on (Sharpe 0.68 vs SPY's 0.35, and a third of the drawdown). But on 2015-2020 it made 3.1%/yr
  against SPY's 12.7%, and its Sharpe was half of SPY's. That gap is overfitting plus a bull market the rules can't keep up with.
- The structural cause is low exposure: about 50% invested on average, because positions are short-term,
  capped at 25%, and stopped out often. In a rising market that costs most of the upside.
- **What the backtest supports changing live today:** dip-buying in all regimes (sized up when VIX >= 22), wider
  2.5-ATR stops, keep `defensive-leader-pullback` in risk-off, drop `inverse-index-trend`, and retire the
  breadth-pause rule from 11-17. The backtest doesn't support it, and it was based on only 2 trades.

## 5. Next steps (before running TEST)
1. Raise time-in-market without losing the drawdown control: hold dip-buys longer, and add a core trend position.
2. Re-tune only on TRAIN, confirm on VALIDATE, then run TEST **once** (`--test`) and report it whatever it shows.
3. Live paper trading (through 12-31) is the forward test of whatever survives.
